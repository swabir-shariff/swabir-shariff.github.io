A typical engagement

An advisory engagement runs in four stages: the first three run over 8–12 weeks, and I return at day 90 for the results review.

01 · Discovery & program assessment

Site visits and structured interviews that establish who is being reached, who is being missed, and why.

02 · Redesign strategy

A written, board-ready plan setting out staffing, scheduling and program-model changes in plain language.

03 · Implementation support

Two working sessions a month to support implementation and resolve issues as they arise.

04 · Day-90 results review

After the engagement, we meet again to review what moved, what did not, and what happens next.

Signature talks

“Don’t offer young people a seat at the table, or a key to one room — hand them the keys to the whole house: the work is theirs to lead, with mentors who look like them behind them.”

All three talks are keynotes — also available as a fireside conversation or panel, virtual or in-person.

Signature Talk · 01

“Keys to the Whole House”

How giving young people real authority over their work builds the trust institutions struggle to earn — a working model for shared decision-making, drawn from The Good Guides.

Best for: youth-serving organisations, funders, public institutions.

Key takeaways

  • Young people need real authority over the work, with support behind them
  • A practical way to share decision-making with the people a program serves
  • An honest account of what happens to trust when decision-making is shared
Signature Talk · 02

“Change the Starting Line”

Redesign where people start, rather than demanding more effort from the people that starting line already failed. A talk for leaders ready to find the starting lines inside their own systems — and change them.

Best for: conferences, education leaders and civic audiences.

Key takeaways

  • A way to spot the starting lines hidden in your own intake, referral and eligibility rules
  • A framework for redesigning where people start inside your own system
  • A worked example: one intake rule, redesigned — and what changed for the people it had been failing
Signature Talk · 03

“Every Piece of the Whole”

Teams underperform when some roles are treated as support instead of as part of the result. This talk shows leaders how to redesign those roles so every person owns a real piece of the work, as they do at The Good Guides.

Best for: leadership teams and boards.

Key takeaways

  • A simple test for whether every role in the room carries real work — and what to change when one doesn’t
  • How to lead so each person’s contribution lands as part of the result
  • How to spot the ‘passenger’ roles in your structure — and the conversation that turns them into ownership

About

Born and raised in Scarborough, I grew up in Toronto Community Housing and watched friends — and my own brother — pulled toward the justice system. The services meant to reach them never did: no mentors, no one they could see themselves in, only solutions designed from the outside. That failure is where my work begins.

Through The Good Guides — the organisation I founded in 2020 and lead as Executive Director today — I lead mentorship, youth leadership and violence-prevention programming for young people aged 11–18, delivered through specific schools and cohorts across Toronto. That work includes school-based violence prevention delivered with community partners and funded by the City of Toronto, alongside sports and co-op placements designed to carry young people into leadership.

I am an operator, not a theorist: accountable for governance, delivery and results. I built the organisation unpaid alongside an executive security career. As Director of Security, I led round-the-clock (24/7) operations for a major retail centre. The Good Guides has operated on steady funding since 2023.

I have also sat at the policy table: I helped lead the City of Toronto Youth Violence Prevention partnership work in Scarborough and helped lead Scarborough's youth engagement through COVID, and I was instrumental in creating Community Connect, a program connecting young people with their neighbourhood officers through certification workshops including CPR, food handling and babysitting. In October 2026, that record was recognised in London, where I was received at St James's Palace.
